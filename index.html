The whole skin, mapped as one body, followed over years.

DermGraph is a research program in dermatologic imaging. It treats the skin surface as a single anatomic model and every visible spot as a point on it, so change can be measured instead of guessed.

The idea

A skin examination usually looks at lesions one at a time. DermGraph looks at the whole surface at once: where each spot sits on the body, which spots are its neighbours, and how the pattern changes from one visit to the next.

One body model

Anatomic landmarks such as the eyes, ears, spine and nipples anchor a single model of each person at each visit. Each spot is placed on that model, not just on a photograph.

Spots as a constellation

Neighbouring spots form a pattern that is unique to the person. The pattern helps confirm where each spot is and shows when something new appears.

Change over time

Lesions are tracked across serial scans. Growth is compared against the same person's other lesions, so each patient serves as their own baseline.

Approach

Total-body photography

A purpose-built booth photographs the whole body from fixed, calibrated positions. Scale comes from the booth itself, so features can be measured in millimetres.

Anatomic site standard

Every finding is coded to a hierarchical body-site vocabulary so results can be compared between visits, between patients and with clinical records.

Linked to dermoscopy and pathology

Close-up dermoscopy and biopsy results are joined to the same spot on the body model, connecting what the scan saw with what was diagnosed.

History

  • 2000Patent application filed for an apparatus for total immersion photography: an enclosure with multiple calibrated cameras and lights for whole-body skin imaging.
  • 2008US Patent 7,359,748 issued, "Apparatus for total immersion photography".
  • TodaySerial total-body scans of a dermatology practice's patients, followed visit to visit and linked to the clinical record, form the basis of the program.

Collaborate

For researchers, clinicians and partners

We welcome work on skin-surface mapping, anatomic standards, longitudinal lesion analysis and the clinical use of total-body photography. Patient data stays within the practice and is never shared through this site.
